How to play

Cyclic — nobody can draw

  1. You only ever have three marks on the board.
  2. Place a fourth and your oldest one vanishes in a puff of smoke.
  3. The mark about to go is shown faded, so you always know what you are giving up.
  4. Three in a row still wins — but the board never fills, so a draw is impossible.
  5. If nobody has forced it after 60 moves, the game is called a draw anyway.

This is the cure for Classic 3×3 always ending level.

Misère — everything backwards

  1. Making three in a row loses you the game.
  2. So you are trying to force the other player into a line they cannot avoid.
  3. On the big board it is the same idea one level up: claiming three mini-boards in a line loses. Mini-boards are still won normally.

Much harder than it sounds — every instinct you have is now wrong.

Classic 3×3 is the game everybody already knows.

All the rules

  1. The board is a single 3×3 grid — nine squares.
  2. Players take turns putting their symbol in any empty square.
  3. Get three of your symbol in a row — across, down or diagonally — and you win.
  4. If all nine squares fill with nobody making a line, it is a draw.

Finding your way

  1. The glowing pill, the raised player card and the board's glow all show whose turn it is.
  2. Taken squares are locked — clicking one shakes the board at you.

Fair warning: two players who both know what they are doing will draw every single time. That is exactly why Super Tic Tac Toe exists.

Super Tic Tac Toe is nine games of noughts and crosses inside one big one.

The rule that catches everyone out

✕

You play the top-right square…

➜

…so they must play in the top-right board.

The square you pick chooses their board. That is the whole game.

All the rules

  1. The big board holds nine mini-boards, each a 3×3 grid — 81 squares in all.
  2. The first player may play anywhere on the board.
  3. The square you play sends your opponent to the matching mini-board.
  4. If that mini-board is already won or full, your opponent gets a free move — any empty square in any board still in play.
  5. Three in a row inside a mini-board claims it. That board then locks.
  6. A mini-board that fills up with nobody winning is dead — it counts for neither side.
  7. Claim three mini-boards in a line to win the whole game.
  8. If every mini-board is decided and no line was made, the game is a draw.
  9. You can never play in a taken square, a locked board, or a board other than the live one.

Finding your way

  1. The mini-board you must play in is the bright one, ringed and cornered in the current colour.
  2. Everything you cannot use is dimmed and blurred out.
  3. When Play anywhere shows, every open board is up for grabs.
  4. The glowing pill at the top, the raised player card and the board's own glow all show whose turn it is.
